# Historical GIS of Ceylon

Open [ceylon-history.qgz](ceylon-history.qgz) in QGIS. The [GeoPackage](ceylon-history.gpkg) contains the vectors and tables. The project also opens the existing 1893 and 1936 georeferenced rasters by relative path. In QGIS, identify a town, district, road or railway and open its form to find the related observations. To compare map traces, enable an earlier map layer in **Earlier map traces and control points**. The two georeferenced rasters start off so the layers are easy to see.

Turn on **Historic sites → Historic sites (reviewed Wikidata points)** for the curated points. Turn on **Modern elevation reference** and zoom in to inspect NSDI terrain context.

## Feature IDs and dates

- Join a census observation's `feature_id` to the same field in `census_units`, `urban_census`, `province_census` or `district_census_1946`. The 1946 province layer shares province IDs with the 1936 proxy. Use `qid` to link the named place into the Ceylon spine, Colonial Office List graph and Empire Evolution. A QID remains independent of our feature ID. The combined Wattala–Mabole–Peliyagoda, Hatton–Dickoya and Tangalla–Beliatta census areas have `anchor_qid` for the representative town. A few historical district and urban scopes have no confirmed entity QID.
- The 42 urban census symbol locations use the completed local [Sri Lanka Wikidata gazetteer](../../spine/gazetteer/sri_lanka_wikidata.jsonl), read on 2026-11-16 with 14,209 resolved items. Each symbol was checked against the place's GeoNames ID. It represents the modern place. Historical count boundaries can differ from modern administrative boundaries.
- `represented_date` is the event, census or map date. `published_date` is the date of the source that reports it, at the known precision. For example, the census took place on 19 March 1946, the preliminary totals appeared on 23 March 1946 and the final report appeared in August 1950. The district map was printed in July 1950 and represents 1946 by context. A blank publication or geometry date means that it could not be established from the source. For modern point locations, the gazetteer snapshot date is recorded separately.
- Geometry rows carry `geometry_source_id`, a locator and a URL. Where a historical source supplies the name or date and another source supplies geometry, both appear on the row. The OSM layers also preserve OSM way IDs and snapshot information. A road candidate's `feature_id` identifies its historical corridor, way and segment endpoints. `osm_feature_id` keeps the upstream identifier, which can repeat across candidates cut from one way.
- `transport_evidence` uses `evidence_id` and `section_id`. `transport_evidence_links` joins evidence to map and OSM corridors via `corridor_id`. A corridor link can be to part of the route or a work on it. `corridor_qid` identifies the named railway line when a feature shows a section of that line. A mapped road class, service date and construction date have distinct source records.

## Census

`census_observations` stores one row per unit, date, metric and source. Figures include persons, male and female counts, occupied houses, habitable buildings, literacy counts, published area and population density. The table retains the printed value and unit. The retrospective DCS series is reported in thousands and carries 100-person resolution. The final 1946 revenue district population totals from Table 97 are derived from the two published sex counts and marked accordingly.

- The 1891 volume gives 1881 and 1891 counts, area and density. It distinguishes Negombo town's old and contemporary Local Board areas, Colombo Municipality from the district outside it, and Vavuniya from Mullaitivu. The 1901 volume includes comparisons from 1871 and adjusts earlier figures to the 1901 boundaries where possible. Original and adjusted counts can coexist. For instance, the 1891 report gives 20,375 people in Kandy Municipality and the 1901 comparison gives 22,801 for 1891. [1891 Table III](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.4536/4536.pdf#page=17), [1901 Table IV](https://digitalcollections.crl.edu/record/387349/files/024.pdf).
- The 1911 town tables provide selected 1901 and 1911 houses, population and literacy counts. The 1921 volume gives province and Colombo Municipality figures for 1911 and 1921, with population by sex, literacy and habitable buildings. The source adjusts 1911 province figures for area changes where necessary. The 1921 island number includes 743 people enumerated on trains and eight at the Basses outside the provincial subtotal. [1911 town tables](https://archive.org/details/tdl.45514-the-census-of-ceylon-1911-town-and-village-statistics-the-towns-and-vi), [1921 town and village volume](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.196126), [DCS historical district table](https://www.statistics.gov.lk/abstract2023/CHAP2/2.1.pdf).
- The March 1946 Gazette gives 1931 comparators and provisional 1946 counts. The final report gives 1946 population, area and density for nine provinces and 42 towns, plus sex counts for 20 revenue districts. Trincomalee town's 1946 count changed from 28,334 to 32,507. The scan check confirmed the provisional Kurunegala count of 485,366, which reconciles with the printed North Western total. [Gazette pp. 489–490](https://diglib.natlib.lk/bitstream/handle/123456789/30704/037_1946-JANUARY-JUNE_9533_I_E.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1), [Final Tables 10, 14 and 97](https://noolaham.net/project/1018/101739/101739.pdf#page=104).
- `headmen_divisions_1934` holds 109 polygons for the 112 Chief Headmen's divisions numbered in the 1935 malaria report maps (six cells carry combined numbers as printed; five small town or coastal cells have no number). Lines come from Map 7 and numbers and names from Map 17 and its key. The plate is 24 miles to an inch, so lines are good to a few km, and the two maps differ by 5–13 km round the Batticaloa lagoon and in parts of Matara, Hambantota and Ratnapura. `identity_confidence` and `note` say how each division was identified. The death-rate classes on Maps 17–19 are not transcribed. [Method](headmen_divisions_1934_method.md); working tables are in `headmen_1934_work/`.
- `district_census_1946_join` is for point-in-polygon joins: each traced district grown 5 km seaward, never into another district. The traced coast is simplified and fitted to about 3 km, so Hambantota, Trincomalee and Tangalla fall outside the traced polygons. Inland boundaries are unchanged, and Avissawella and Hatton-Dickoya still fall in the neighbouring district. For towns, use `urban_census.district_1946`, the district printed in the census table.
- `province_census` (the approximate 1936 provinces) is cut to the 1946 traced land plus 5 km. The modern administrative areas it came from ran far out to sea.
- In `rail_1936_osm`, `gauge` is the modern OSM tag; `gauge_mm_1936` gives 762 mm for the Kelani Valley line. The layer has no geometry for Avissawella–Yatiyantota, Avissawella–Opanake or Nanu Oya–Ragalla.
- Three amenity places use a GeoNames point because the Wikidata coordinate was several km out (Kitulgala by 48 km); `coordinate_note` says so. `ferguson_towns_1893.reference_offset_m` gives the measured offset for the seven towns checked.
- `district_census_1946` traces 20 district polygons from the official report's map. Chilaw and Puttalam are separate. The combined Gazette tally and earlier district definitions have their own census records. The shared-edge district trace had a 3.41 km leave-one-out RMS fit error, with larger errors at three possible check locations. The nine 1946 provinces are assembled from the traced districts. A second province layer reuses the approximate 1936 OSM-derived polygons, including some offshore extents. Use source-reported areas for census density. [1946 districts sheet](https://noolaham.net/project/1018/101739/101739.pdf#page=59), [District trace method](district_1946_method.md).

Independent reviewers checked the source scans behind all 1,900 observations (1,860 transcribed values and 40 derived district totals). The audit corrected Kurunegala's provisional 1946 population to **485,366** and Chilaw Local Board's 1891 occupied houses to **694**. No observation ID changed. See image receipts for [1891](census_audit_1891.md), [1901](census_audit_1901.md), [1911 and 1921](census_audit_1911_1921.md) and [the Gazette, final 1946 report and DCS](census_audit_late.md).

Run `python3 audit_census.py --output census_reconciliation.tsv` from this directory to reproduce the [605 arithmetic checks](census_reconciliation.tsv). The same checks are in QGIS under **Sources and evidence tables → Census sums and exceptions for review**. The script records each input and page beside the subtotal. It checks sex counts, provincial and district subtotals, the combined 1921 train and Basses figures, and rounded series at their displayed precision. Use `--review-only` for the [exception register](census_reconciliation_review.tsv): the provisional Gazette and final 1946 report both put the 1931 island total eight above the sum of the provinces; the 1891 Chilaw area cell is illegible and remains blank. Arithmetic checks supplement the image review. They cannot establish that a figure was transcribed correctly if several errors happen to balance.

## Historic sites and elevation

`historical_wikidata_places` contains 39 reviewed ancient and colonial landmarks, from prehistoric cave sites and historic temples to fortifications, botanic gardens, the Hantane Tea Factory and railway stations. The [selection table](historical_places.tsv) preserves QIDs, modern coordinates and Wikidata labels where available. Each historical role and its date or period has a separate source locator and link. The points are representative; locality, institution and station reference points cannot locate a former structure or its footprint. The Wikidata snapshot date is separate from the historical date and the publication date. A documented but displaced point, such as the local Hakgala Botanic Garden coordinate or the duplicate Matara Fort coordinate at Star Fort, was held back pending correction. The point layer is a curated starting set rather than an inventory of all monuments.

The [1834 Gazetteer review](gazetteer_1834_review.md) compares the later Navrang OCR with the original 1834 scans. The `gazetteer_1834_attestations` table retains historical aliases, road and ferry references and Chitty's attributed 1814 Cottiar population. The report uses original-page links. This is evidence for the named settlement or route. The QGIS layer **Casie Chitty Gazetteer, 1834 (grounded places)** under **Historic sites** maps 189 entries using documented modern reference points from Wikidata, OpenStreetMap, and GeoNames. **Casie Chitty Gazetteer entry index** includes 439 entries and records which ones have a point. Join on the stable `entry_id` and use Wikidata `qid` where supplied. [Reviewable grounding decisions](../../spine/analysis/hgis_chitty_1834_grounded.tsv), [entry index](../../spine/analysis/hgis_chitty_1834_index.tsv).

The [NSDI elevation service](https://gisapps.nsdi.gov.lk/server/services/SLNSDI/Elevation/MapServer/WMSServer?SERVICE=WMS&VERSION=1.3.0&REQUEST=GetCapabilities) is an optional modern WMS for visual comparison. Contours are available around 1:189,000 or closer and spot heights and elevation regime around 1:23,600 or closer. The service returns map images. Use an elevation raster for slope or height calculations.

**Relief shading, SRTM 30 m** is drawn over the other layers in the QGIS project, and **Elevation, SRTM 30 m (metres)** sits under *Modern elevation reference* for heights and slopes. Both cover the whole island. They come from 15 Tilezen terrain tiles (SRTM, surveyed February 2000) downloaded on 17 November 2026 to `/mnt/data/jic823/ceylon_hgis_elevation/srtm_30m`, where `README.txt` gives the source. The surface is modern: reservoirs built after the dates of the historical layers are in it. [Tile sources and attribution](https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/attribution.md)

The separate local VæSSA raster is an optional modern terrain layer under the same group. Its file header places it in eastern Sri Lanka near Ampara (approximately 81.64–81.82° E and 7.18–7.36° N), agreeing with the precise product sheet. The catalog's combined summary mentions western river catchments; that description does not locate this specific file. It has a 0.5 m grid in SLD99. The supplier reports vertical accuracy of about 2 m, so the grid size does not imply 0.5 m height accuracy. The data and overview files are kept together at `/mnt/data/jic823/ceylon_hgis_elevation/vaessa_0_5m`. [Dataset catalog and license](https://energydata.info/dataset/sri-lanka-digital-elevation-model) · [Precise DEM product sheet](https://datacatalogfiles.worldbank.org/ddh-published/0039990/1/DR0049797/c2_vaessa_product_sheet_precisedem.pdf)

## Roads and railways

Map records use the earliest checked depiction as evidence. A map may show a route before official traffic began. A name or route on one map can also persist through later bridge replacements and realignments. Use an OSM alignment for historical analysis only where fixed landmarks, source-map detail or construction records support that placement. The original 1936 transport project and layers remain available at [motor-map-1936](../motor-map-1936/).

The independent [road and ferry review](transport_qa.md) and [railway review](railway_qa.md) identify remaining alignment, junction, missing branch and chronology issues with original scan locations and OSM way IDs.

- **Road maps.** The 1893 Ferguson layer includes eleven selected sections. The lines follow the printed principal-road symbols where legible. The sheet shows additional roads. Georeferencing and paper folds can move the traces relative to modern reference data. In 1848 Skinner described the Avisawella–Ratnapura road as precarious, with deteriorated bridges and drains. The 1915 Railway Board map adds schematic road evidence on nine corridors, including the routes through Dambulla and the Trincomalee–Batticaloa coast. The Dawson Motor Map public preview is catalogued ca. 1930. It corroborates selected corridors in the south and east at regional scale. Its resolution limits individual junction and ferry identification. The 1936 map provides 82 traced roads and 11 motor-ferry links. [PWD history p. 21](https://noolaham.net/project/961/96086/96086.pdf#page=59), [1893 Ferguson sheet](https://www.davidrumsey.com/luna/servlet/detail/RUMSEY~8~1~338023~90105863), [March 1915 Railway Board map](https://www.davidrumsey.com/luna/servlet/detail/RUMSEY~8~1~328296~90096788), [Dawson Motor Map](https://raremaps.com/gallery/detail/68129/survey-department-motor-map-of-ceylon), [1936 Motor Map](https://www.davidrumsey.com/luna/servlet/detail/RUMSEY~8~1~332276~90100738).
- **Construction and upgrades.** The PWD history gives both 1825 and 1831 for completion of the Colombo–Kandy road, first coach service in 1832 and the start of metalling in 1841. It dates work on Kandy–Nuwara Eliya to 1827–1837. The Blue Book distinguishes new work from improvements, such as the Pesalai–Talaimannar project begun in December 1925, Badulla–Batticaloa widening in 1926 and 1927, and expenditure on a deviation of the Tangalla–Hambantota–Liyangahatota road. The 1928 Andala–Tissa expenditure identifies a local work project. Its exact tie-in to the traced 1936 roads is unresolved. Annual expenditure and original estimates have separate fields. [PWD history p. 11 and chronology](https://noolaham.net/project/961/96086/96086.pdf#page=45), [1928 Blue Book, E 16, E 18 and DD 1](https://books.google.com/books?id=0_hRAQAAMAAJ).
- **Road usability and crossings.** The 1928 Blue Book has 483 provincial road entries totalling the printed 4,978.22 miles, plus 16 principal route summaries that overlap the inventory. Use `table_scope` when aggregating. Its symbols change meaning on different pages. `traffic_class` decodes them using the page-specific key and `note` preserves exceptions. For Manampitiya–Vakaneri–Valaichchenai, DD 3 lists 31 miles and marks the route unsuitable for motor cars. The 1925 Gazette has a Class II crossing named Valaichchenai to Polonnaruwa at mile 86, chain 60. It supplies the historical chainage, and the exact location and through alignment have not been established. `road_rail_crossings_1925` also preserves one other named Class II crossing, eleven Class III cart tracks, and the named Trincomalee–Batticaloa crossing. [Blue Book DD 3](https://books.google.com/books?id=0_hRAQAAMAAJ), [Gazette pp. 2026–2027](https://diglib.natlib.lk/bitstream/handle/123456789/23573/014_1925-JULY-SEPTEMBER_7481_E.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1#page=4).
- **Rail stages.** Colombo–Ambepussa had a first special train in December 1864 and opened to regular traffic in October 1865. The first Kandy train ran in April 1867, with regular traffic in August. Railway reports distinguish construction and service in the 1920s. Gal-oya–Trincomalee opened to goods on 19 January 1927 and all traffic on 2 May 1927. Polonnaruwa–Batticaloa opened to goods on 4 January 1928 and all traffic on 12 March 1928. The narrow-gauge opening table gives distinct dates, lengths and some original costs for the Kelani Valley and Uda Pussellawa extensions. [PWD chronology](https://noolaham.net/project/961/96086/96086.pdf#page=22), [CGR narrow-gauge opening table p. 66](https://rasikawick.lk/content/downloads/NGRC%20-%20Supplement%20One.pdf#page=66), [1927 annual report, Trincomalee](https://archive.org/download/colonial-reports-1927-1378/ColonialReports1927-1378.pdf#page=1033), [1927 annual report, Batticaloa](https://archive.org/download/colonial-reports-1927-1378/ColonialReports1927-1378.pdf#page=944).
- **OSM comparison.** The 1915 sheet depicts the rail route as far as Chilaw, while the government report dates goods service there to March 1916 and opening to all traffic to 1 May. A verified 67.041 km modern path connects the Ragama and Chilaw stop nodes through 16 OSM ways. This is a comparison alignment. The 1915 traces themselves use the [source crop](railway-map-1915-detail.jpg), hand-picked source pixels and a quadratic fit to 18 GeoNames anchors. Leave-one-out RMS error is about 11.6 km. The controls, per-point errors and traces are in the GeoPackage. Use the crop for what the map depicted, the printed reports for service dates and the OSM candidate for present-day detailed geometry. [1916 annual report p. 30](https://noolaham.net/project/970/96999/96999.pdf#page=36), [OSM attribution and licensing](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright).

## Reviewed bridges

`reviewed_historical_bridges` contains two bounded crossing geometries. At Mawanella, the 1936 map, PWD history and Provincial Council record support identifying OSM way 316359067 as the surviving Maha Oya brick bridge. Only the 78 m span was reviewed. At Batticaloa, the 1928 report dates the opening of the Lady Manning Bridge and its approaches to 9 October. The 1936 inset labels it. The traced 281 m deck comes from 2024 orthoreferenced imagery and has separate source and coordinate dates. Engineering plans and a 2025 inspection distinguish it from the newer parallel bridge. The reviewed alignments end at the bridges, and historical approaches have yet to be verified. [Alignment review](road_alignment_review.tsv), [Lady Manning digitizing method](lady_manning_bridge_method.md), [1928 annual report](https://archive.org/download/colonial-reports-1928-1455/ColonialReports1928-1455.pdf#page=99), [Mawanella bridge record](https://tourism.sg.gov.lk/the-brick-bridge-at-mawanella/).

## 1936 transport amenities

The map's marginal tables supply 329 listings for petrol depot localities, rest houses and railway stations that accepted motor-car bookings. Of these, 249 have representative points. The dataset retains the printed names, class marks and capacities as dated observations. A listed locality may represent multiple facilities. The mapped points use Wikidata settlement or station coordinates, and unmatched names remain in the source table. Bedrooms and beds are blank for the two grouped rest-house entries at Negombo and Trincomalee. [1936 map, left margin](https://www.davidrumsey.com/luna/servlet/detail/RUMSEY~8~1~332276~90100738).

## Reproduce or query

`sources.tsv` and `railway_later_sources.tsv` contain the source catalogue. The TSV inputs preserve source spellings, locators and the evidence links. The [1891 method](census_1891_method.md) and [1901 method](census_1901_method.md) describe the boundary adjustments. For the 1915 crop, use `gdal_translate -srcwin 8800 17700 2900 3750 -outsize 1100 0 -of JPEG 13440000.jp2 crop.jpg`. The table `rail_1915_controls` contains the spatial residuals and the TSVs preserve the original pixel positions.

Run from this directory using the system Python with GDAL/OGR, GEOS, NumPy and PyQGIS. The generated district and rail comparison files and the amenity place matches are already included. Rebuild them when revising their inputs.

```sh
/usr/bin/python3 district_1946_build.py
/usr/bin/python3 build_rail_1915_osm.py
/usr/bin/python3 match_motor_places.py
/usr/bin/python3 build.py
QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen /usr/bin/python3 build_project.py
```

For example, this query returns the publication-specific population figures and locators for Trincomalee town.

```sql
SELECT represented_date, value, status, published_date, locator, source_url
FROM census_observations
WHERE feature_id = 'urban:trincomalee' AND metric = 'population'
ORDER BY represented_date, published_date
```

The [1924 one-inch topographic series](https://www.survey.gov.lk/sdweb/page_content_about_us.php?id=56409db7884ae2cee43caa68ac150b555ec3af42) and [alternate Motor Map editions](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002245033) are prospective higher-resolution sources for local roads. The 1887 and 1901 Stanford previews were screened as context. Their available scans did not support reading closely spaced road, rail and administrative lines reliably enough for new corridor claims.

# Census of Ceylon, 1901

P. Arunachalam’s *The Census of Ceylon, 1901*, Volume II, was printed by H. C. Cottle, Acting Government Printer, in Colombo in 1902. The column dates correspond to the four censuses of 27 March 1871, 17 February 1881, 26 February 1891 and 1 March 1901. [Title page](https://digitalcollections.crl.edu/record/387349/files/002.pdf)

`census_1901_comparison.tsv` transcribes occupied houses, enumerated persons, males and females from Table IV, pp. 24–29. It includes the island, all nine provinces, all 21 district rows, and 18 municipality, local board and other named town-scope rows. The report excludes the military, shipping and prisoners of war. It adjusts the 1871–1891 figures as far as possible to divisions as constituted on 1 March 1901. A blank keeps a town value unassigned when the original uses braces to report a combined area. [Table IV title and note](https://digitalcollections.crl.edu/record/387349/files/023.pdf) · [pp. 24–25](https://digitalcollections.crl.edu/record/387349/files/024.pdf) · [pp. 26–27](https://digitalcollections.crl.edu/record/387349/files/025.pdf) · [pp. 28–29](https://digitalcollections.crl.edu/record/387349/files/026.pdf)

The 1901 publication is a distinct series from the 1891 Volume II census figures. For example, its adjusted 1891 comparison gives 22,801 for Kandy Municipality, while the 1891 census tables give 20,375. The 1901 report explicitly identifies territory changes in Jaffna, Mannar and Mullaittivu following the abolition of Vavuniya District. Vavuniya North and South appear under Mullaittivu District as subdivisions in the 1901 tables. The district identifier links this historical named unit. It carries no claim that the census distribution follows the 1946 geometry. Table IV identifies Colombo Municipality, the remainder of Colombo District, and Negombo District separately. [pp. 24–25](https://digitalcollections.crl.edu/record/387349/files/024.pdf) · [p. 27](https://digitalcollections.crl.edu/record/387349/files/025.pdf)

All transcribed persons counts equal the sum of the male and female components. All printed province figures reconcile with their district components, and their sums reconcile with the printed island totals for each year and metric. The 1901 Table IV count for Matara Local Board is 11,848. The 1911 census town table prints 11,880 for its 1901 comparison, and both can be retained with their separate sources.

# Lady Manning Bridge centerline

The 1936 Survey Department Motor Map labels Lady Manning Bridge on the Batticaloa inset. In the 8410 × 11077 source scan the crossing lies around x7685–7756, y5040–5078. The *Ceylon Annual General Report for 1928* records that the governor opened the Kalladi bridge and road approaches on October 9, 1928. It describes six 150 ft spans and a design that could carry rail and road traffic. The rail provision was conditional on future extension south of Batticaloa. [1936 motor map, Rumsey 10791.002](https://www.davidrumsey.com/luna/servlet/detail/RUMSEY~8~1~332276~90100738) · [1928 annual report, printed p. 40](https://archive.org/download/colonial-reports-1928-1455/ColonialReports1928-1455.pdf#page=99)

The 2006 JICA study places the existing steel truss on the seaward side of the proposed parallel bridge in Figure 13.1. The Road Development Authority and Italian Civil Protection team photographed both decks during its December 17, 2025 inspection. The old bridge is the narrow visible truss northeast of the wider concrete deck. The inspection describes its total length as approximately 300 m. [JICA executive summary, printed pp. 43–44](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/11829124_02.pdf#page=24) · [Old Kallady Bridge inspection, printed p. 45](https://www.protezionecivile.gov.it/static/6425ca939c7a070d8aa5616d60ca8eb7/advisory-mission-sri-lanka-2025-final-report-070126-hr.pdf#page=46)

[The GeoJSON](lady_manning_bridge.geojson) represents the surviving deck. The map establishes its historical identity, and the satellite export supplies its coordinates. The manually selected endpoints lie at the visible abutments where the truss meets the approach surfaces. The resulting two-point line is about 281 m long in WGS 84 / UTM zone 44N. It is a standalone bridge feature. Connections to the 1936 road network and the approach footprints require separate evidence.

## Reproduce the geometry

1. Open the JSON response from the [Esri World Imagery export](https://server.arcgisonline.com/ArcGIS/rest/services/World_Imagery/MapServer/export?bbox=81.7045%2C7.7174%2C81.7105%2C7.7210&bboxSR=4326&imageSR=4326&size=1200%2C720&format=png32&f=json) and fetch the PNG at its `href`. The response gives the image dimensions and the georeferenced extent. It returned a 1200 × 720 image and bounds `[81.7045, 7.7174, 81.7105, 7.7210]` in EPSG:4326, with the bounds expressed as longitude and latitude. The PNG retrieved on November 16, 2026 had SHA-256 `d382d5b98118dbb7f751c7e7e9086f58e143d1860f0cb549299e2d5511320580`. The service can refresh its imagery.
2. Use pixel centers `(339, 202)` at the northwest abutment and `(786, 446)` at the southeast abutment, measured in zero-based columns and rows from the upper left.
3. Compute longitude as `81.7045 + (column + 0.5) * 0.000005` and latitude as `7.7210 - (row + 0.5) * 0.000005`. The increment is the extent divided by the number of pixels in each dimension. The GeoJSON uses longitude, then latitude.

The [imagery metadata lookup](https://server.arcgisonline.com/ArcGIS/rest/services/World_Imagery/MapServer/identify?f=pjson&geometry=81.7073%2C7.71935&geometryType=esriGeometryPoint&sr=4326&mapExtent=81.7045%2C7.7174%2C81.7105%2C7.7210&imageDisplay=1200%2C720%2C96&tolerance=1&returnGeometry=false&layers=all) identifies Vantor Vivid Advanced imagery, captured August 28, 2024, with ground resolution 0.46 m and reported positional accuracy 8.47 m. Endpoint selection adds visual uncertainty. Coordinate decimals allow the pixel conversion to be reproduced. At midspan the digitized line is roughly 13 m and 18 m from the two present A4 OSM carriageways. The separate structures are visible in the same image, and the deck attribution is independently corroborated by the engineering study and inspection photographs.

The [alignment review](road_alignment_review.tsv) identifies the replacement-deck OSM ways and the bridge footprint that carries the historic bridge's Wikidata association. The GeoJSON coordinates come from the Esri/Vantor image export.

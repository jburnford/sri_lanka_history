# Transport geometry review

This review compared the 1936 Survey Department Motor Map with the delivered GeoPackages and the November 12, 2026 local OSM snapshot. Approximate source pixels are columns and rows in the 8,410 × 11,077 original scan, with the origin at the upper left. Modern coordinates below are longitude, latitude. The image overlays use the project's two-dimensional fit and retain its local map distortion. The raw sheet, named places, water crossings and junction patterns determined the readings. [1936 source map, Rumsey 10791.002](https://www.davidrumsey.com/luna/servlet/detail/RUMSEY~8~1~332276~90100738) · [QA scan and overlay samples](transport_qa_samples.jpg)

## Rail findings

- The modern rail layer `rail_1936_osm` (named `rails_1936_osm` in the motor-map package) contains 653 source OSM ways. Every coordinate sequence matches the exact local reference. The 16,874 unique track nodes form **one** undirected component through shared OSM node IDs, so the selected surviving routes have no topological gap between them. Parallel tracks add to the summed way length. This topology result covers present-day ways, including reconstructed and re-gauged track. Historic line extents need the map and operational sources.
- **Uda Pussellawa Railway is absent.** The 1936 original shows railway from Nanu Oya through Nuwara Eliya and Kandapola to Ragalla, in the window x4650–5600, y7350–8120. The black/white railway line visibly separates from the black selected motor road in and east of Nuwara Eliya, winding around Kandapola toward the Ragalla symbol. Local OSM near Nuwara Eliya includes unrelated miniature railway ways `1196870202`, `1196870203`, `1196870207`, `1196870210`. Trace the 1936 printed corridor separately, starting with the Nuwara Eliya inset and the earlier schematic feature `1915-rail:uda` for identity. Opening section records `s-nuwara`, `s-kandapola`, `s-ragalla` give 1903–1904 dates. [Narrow Gauge Railways of Ceylon, Supplement One, p. 66](https://rasikawick.lk/content/downloads/NGRC%20-%20Supplement%20One.pdf#page=66)
- **Both former Kelani Valley branches are absent.** The 1936 sheet shows Avissawella–Yatiyantota nearing the Yatiyantota symbol at approximately (3747, 7658), alongside the thick road but with the white slot rail symbol. It also shows Avissawella–Ratnapura–Dela–Opanayake along x3450–4570, y7820–8820. The retained modern rail ends at Avissawella, including OSM `404694650` (80.208356, 6.954475 at its end). Abandoned bridge ways `550674269` (80.414610, 6.682407) and `550887305` (80.431608, 6.664963) are possible separate anchors on the latter route and need remnant-specific confirmation. Trace the original map without drawing current OSM connections across the removed network. The comparison features `1915-rail:yatiyantota` and `1915-rail:kv-east` show the earlier extents; the latter ends at Ratnapura in 1915.
- **Talaimannar historic pier approach is absent beyond the surviving connected rail.** The original railway bends north to the pier symbol near (2251, 2325). Retained way `589069455` starts at node `1422426723` (79.730591, 9.107419), short of the pier. The local extract has abandoned ways `1210622572` and `1493149034` with shared node `8090201402`, reaching (79.730203, 9.109841). They are plausible separately mapped pier remnants consistent with the original. Their nodes do not attach to the retained line. Record the distinction from former service and the separate ferry.
- **Batticaloa main-line comparison.** The retained terminus approach includes `1228988154` (historical package feature ID `99628395-6a5b-57c8-b658-25d6368a0109`). It is part of the connected surviving graph. The 1936 map inset shows the railway separately from the Lady Manning road bridge. The 1928 report describes future rail provision on that bridge as conditional. A road crossing must not become a rail edge based on that provision. [1928 annual general report, printed p. 40](https://archive.org/download/colonial-reports-1928-1455/ColonialReports1928-1455.pdf#page=99)

## Cross-checks and limits

- For each of the 2,642 modern road candidate features, I checked the original reference geometry ID, OSM way version and the inclusive node and coordinate slices. All match across 2,425 distinct ways. Each nonempty corridor candidate is internally connected by exact OSM node IDs. Their union has 13 components; modern roads and historical ferries can appropriately be separated. Each identification still needs cartographic and structure evidence.
- The historical GIS project displays 1936 map roads and ferries by default and leaves its modern road candidates off. The original motor-map QGIS project instead opens with the candidate layer checked. This presentation can make speculative matches such as the Wirawila detour look historical. Defaulting to the reviewed printed alignment in that project would make inspection clearer.
- Earlier map sample. The 1915 schematic marks the Uda Pussellawa and both Kelani Valley branches in green. Its Trincomalee–Batticaloa road is a thin solid line separate from the bay coastline, supporting the existence of `1915-road:trincomalee-batticaloa` as a schematic corridor. The layer's recorded 11.63 km holdout RMS and the schematic island depiction preclude using its projected lines to locate exact junctions or ferries. The 1915 rail schematic extends to Chilaw ahead of the attested 1916 openings. [March 1915 Bates map](https://www.davidrumsey.com/luna/servlet/detail/RUMSEY~8~1~328296~90096788) · [1916 General Report, PDF p. 36](https://noolaham.net/project/970/96999/96999.pdf#page=36)
- Earlier map sample. The 1893 Ferguson legend explicitly distinguishes railways made, constructing and proposed. Around Weligama–Matara the grain, overlapping coast symbols and parallel route strokes do not establish that the full line was open. The Galle–Matara feature `d48c0ba7-3d38-5406-b879-544983a87731` is already flagged `review required`, and the Sri Lanka Railways chronology gives 1895 for rail reaching Matara. Preserve that uncertain depiction status. [Ferguson map, Rumsey 11597.002](https://www.davidrumsey.com/luna/servlet/detail/RUMSEY~8~1~338023~90105863) · [Sri Lanka Railways chronology, p. 12](https://railway.gov.lk/web/images/pdf/railway_toursim_sri_lanka.pdf#page=12)
- All findings are read-only. No road or rail geometries, QGIS layers, or source inputs were changed. This was a targeted visual audit of the named regions and earlier-layer samples, alongside whole-layer topology and lineage checks.

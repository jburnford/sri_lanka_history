## Defects to repair, in order of size

1. **Sub-entry headings kept too long or lost.** Articles are in alphabetical order; some have sub-entries
   under a heading that ends in a dash ("Seeds—": Gingelly, Mustard, Tea). The reader keeps a heading when it
   should drop it ("Rifles— Books", "Shells— Skins, undressed", "Railway Plant and Rolling Stock— Stationery")
   and loses it when it should keep it (bare "Other", "Tinned", "Free", "Bone", "Gingelly", "Tea" for
   "Tobacco, manufactured— Other", "Fish— Tinned", "Unenumerated— Free", "Manure— Bone", "Seeds— Gingelly",
   "Seeds— Tea"). Known causes: the alphabetical test in `start()` is too weak; and a heading that shares its
   OCR row with "Br. Col.—" or "For. Coun.—" in the country cell (`['Seeds—', 'Br. Col.—', '', ...]`) is not
   treated as a heading. Design a better rule and say what it is. Useful evidence: a top-level article sorts
   after the previous top-level article; a sub-entry sorts after the previous sub-entry of the same heading;
   generic words (Other, Tinned, Fresh, Dyed, Gray, Free, In wood, In bottle, Unenumerated...) are almost
   always sub-entries; the same heading and sub-entries recur in every volume and in both flows, so the
   whole 1901–1906 output can be used to learn which names occur as sub-entries of which heading. Where the
   rule cannot decide, keep the article name as printed and add a note
   `heading uncertain: may belong under "<heading>"`; do not guess silently.
2. **Class and group text fused into the article name** ("III.— Manufactured (3) Other—contd. Jewellery",
   "III.— Bricks and Tiles", "Articles— Yarn" where "Articles—contd." is the tail of the class heading
   "III.—Manufactured Articles—contd."). Peel all of it off into class_as_printed / group_as_printed.
   Also keep class and group current: the reviewer saw stale ones (class "II Raw Materials" on rows of
   "III.—Manufactured Articles").
3. **Articles named by a group heading** ("Cotton" for "(b) Cotton— / Lace and Net"): when the next row
   names an article, that row's name is the article and the group heading is only the group.
4. **Collapsed blocks.** On some pages the OCR put a whole article into one row: the unit cell then holds the
   country list (`cwt. Burma ... " For. Coun.— ...`). Detect this (a unit longer than about 12 characters or
   containing leaders or quote marks) and write the row to the skipped file instead of emitting it.
5. **Units.** Braced pairs ("{ yds." / "{ doz") where one article is given in two units per country: read
   them as two unit lines of the same country if the rows make that clear, otherwise skip the article's rows
   with a reason. A quantity must never be emitted with a unit that is a country fragment or brace.
6. **Country names split over two rows** ("Other British Pos-" / "sessions in Africa ..."): the OCR then
   carries each later country's figures on the row below. Join the two label rows; then check with the
   article's total whether the figures are shifted, and if the article does not add up, add the note
   `possible row shift after a wrapped country name` to its country rows.
7. **Rows that are not data**: page-top lines ("—contd.", "Articles—contd") and "Carried over" / "Brought
   forward" lines must not be emitted as countries. An unlabelled row of figures that stands on a
   "Br. Col.—" or "For. Coun.—" line is a country row whose name was lost, not the article total: skip it
   with that reason.
8. **Government Stores.** After the export ledger some volumes print a second import ledger, "General
   Imports of Government Stores" (1904 pdf pages 711–716, 1905 730–737, 1906 733–738; check the page
   headings in `pages/<year>.jsonl.gz`). Mark those rows by putting `government stores imports` in the note,
   and start articles afresh there.
9. **Tea exports** are printed with two quantity columns of Ceylon produce, Black and Green, so the row has
   one figure more than the layout expects and the later columns shift. Read black and green both as
   `quantity_ceylon_produce` with `component black` / `component green` in the note; use the row's own
   arithmetic (black + green + other = total) to decide which printed figure is which, and skip a row that
   fits no reading. "Tea" under "Seeds—" is tea seed, not tea.
